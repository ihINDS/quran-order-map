Overview

This page looks at how the Quran is put together: the order of its 114 surahs in the mushaf, the order in which they were revealed, and the patterns scholars have found in that arrangement. Blue marks Meccan surahs and gold marks Medinan ones throughout.

It moves through seven parts: the two orders of all 114 surahs, how Meccan and Medinan revelation differ, Islahi's seven blocks that each move from belief to community, a full list of every surah you can sort, al-Fatihah's five stages and their answers, the ring of al-Baqarah, and links between neighbouring surahs. The example verses in each part illustrate its point rather than summarise whole surahs; they are paraphrased, and each links to Quran.com for the Arabic and full translations.

This is not my own work. I gathered the research of classical and modern scholars, linked throughout, to put something useful in one place. The Quran is a miracle, and I believe understanding its structure gives keys to understanding it. I ask Allah for His guidance and His forgiveness for any mistake made here; whatever is correct is from Allah, and any error is my own.

Two phases of the mission

Mecca builds belief. Medina builds a community.

In Mecca the Muslims were a persecuted minority with no state, so revelation shaped what they believed and who they were. In Medina they became a community with a city to run, so revelation added laws, institutions and rules for living together. These are strong tendencies, not rigid rules: Meccan surahs teach ethics, and Medinan surahs still teach belief.

Meccan: belief

c. 610–622 CE · 86 surahs · about 13 years
Focus
Tawhid, the Hereafter, prophethood, and the moral character of the believer
Style
Short, rhythmic verses; oaths such as "By the dawn"; vivid scenes of the Day of Judgement
Address
Often "O mankind"; arguing with the idolaters of Quraysh
Example
112:1–4. Say: He is Allah, One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, and there is none comparable to Him.
Example
99:7–8. Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it.

Medinan: community

622–632 CE · 28 surahs · about 10 years
Focus
Worship as a community, family and inheritance law, money, war and treaties, the hypocrites
Style
Longer verses, detailed rulings, commentary on events such as Badr, Uhud and the Trench
Address
Often "O you who believe"; also the Jews and Christians of Medina
Example
2:183. O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was for those before you, so that you may become mindful of Allah.
Example
49:10. The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your brothers.
Islahi's seven groups

Seven blocks, one repeated movement

The groups follow the mushaf order: each one is a consecutive run of surahs exactly as they sit in the mushaf, from Group I (surahs 1–5) to Group VII (67–114). Each group opens with Meccan surahs that lay down belief and closes with Medinan surahs that build the community on it. The pattern runs seven times, each with a different central theme. Al-Fatihah: five stages, five answers

In a hadith qudsi, the Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah divided the prayer between Himself and His servant in two halves, and answers each verse of al-Fatihah as it is recited. The first half praises Allah, the middle verse is shared, and the last half is the servant's request. The hadith begins from "Praise be to Allah"; the basmalah (1:1) opens the surah before these stages.

Allah's half

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds.
"My servant has praised Me."
Stage 2 · Mercy · 1:3
الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ The Most Merciful, the Ever-Merciful.
"My servant has extolled Me."
Stage 3 · Sovereignty · 1:4
مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ Master of the Day of Judgement.
"My servant has glorified Me."
Between Allah and His servant
Stage 4 · Commitment · 1:5
إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.

The servant's half
Stage 5 · Request · 1:6–7
اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ Guide us to the straight path: the path of those You have blessed, not of those who earned anger, nor of those who went astray.
"This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked for."
The answer, one verse later · 2:2This is the Book, about which there is no doubt, guidance for the God-conscious. The request for guidance is answered by the Quran itself.
The path described · 2:3–5Those who believe in the unseen, establish prayer and spend from what they are given. "They are upon guidance from their Lord": the blessed path, now with a description.
The close of the Quran · 113 & 114The Quran opens by asking for guidance and closes with two prayers for refuge: protection from whatever would pull the guided person off the path.

The ring of al-Baqarah

Al-Baqarah was revealed over roughly nine years, yet its nine sections mirror one another around a centre: the first matches the last, the second matches the second-to-last, and so on. Tap any section to see it beside its mirror. Section boundaries follow Raymond Farrin's analysis and are approximate.

Sources and caveats. Group boundaries and themes follow Islahi's Tadabbur-i-Qur'an as summarised by Shehzad Saleem and Mustansir Mir (Coherence in the Qur'an). Pairs are shown in Islahi's sequential scheme; in a few places he treats a surah as a supplement to a pair rather than a pair member, so check his commentary for the exact pairing inside Groups III–VI. Meccan and Medinan labels and the revelation sequence follow the 1924 Cairo (Al-Azhar) mushaf; that sequence is a traditional reconstruction, and scholars differ on the classification of about a dozen surahs, marked with a dotted underline.
